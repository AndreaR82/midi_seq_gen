melody_model / tokenizer.py

Notes Into Tokens

A hand-rolled tokenizer turns a monophonic melody into a flat sequence of integers a Transformer can predict — and turns the integers back into notes. Here is exactly what each one stands for.

The whole scheme is one line of grammar. A fragment opens with a start marker and a length, then alternates what is sounding with how long it sounds, until it ends.

Everything below is generated from the real vocabulary and the real encode() output — 113 tokens in total, at the default TokenizerConfig.

1 · Time is a grid, not a clock

Before anything is tokenized, a melody is quantized onto a 16th-note grid: steps_per_beat = 4, beats_per_bar = 4, so one bar is 16 steps. Positions and durations are integer step counts, never floats. Here is a two-bar fragment on that grid — six notes and two silences.

2 · Encoding is a cursor walking left to right

encode() holds a cursor at step 0 and sweeps the notes in time order. Every time it meets a note it emits a PITCH_p and a DUR_n; every gap it has to cross first becomes a REST with its own duration. The cursor never jumps — which is why the token stream needs no position numbers at all. Position is implied by everything that came before.

3 · What each token actually represents

Four kinds, and nothing else. A token is never a chord, a velocity, a channel, or an instrument — those live outside the model, in app/.

Laid end to end, that is the model's entire universe: 113 ids. Small vocabularies are the point — a fragment of any length still draws from the same short list, so a ~1–2M-parameter Transformer can learn the distribution from a laptop-sized corpus.

4 · Three rules that fall out of the scheme

Long notes split, then re-merge

The duration vocabulary stops at DUR_32 (two bars). A four-bar drone is emitted as two identical back-to-back pairs; decode()'s _merge_adjacent rejoins same-pitch neighbours into one note. The vocabulary stays tiny no matter how long a note runs.

Out-of-range values clamp, they don't crash

Pitch is clamped to 36–96 (C2–C7), duration to 1–32 steps, length to 1–16 bars. A transposition that would push a note off the top of the range lands on the top of the range instead — the tokenizer always returns a legal sequence.

Decoding tolerates a broken tail

Generated sequences get cut off mid-token by the length limit, so the parser is defensive: it stops at the first EOS, drops a dangling event with no duration, and resyncs past a pair that doesn't type-check rather than throwing.

5 · What the scheme buys, and what it costs

  • Length is a control token, not post-processing. LEN_<bars> sits at position 1, so "give me four bars" is a condition the model was trained on — not a truncation applied afterwards. At generation time every LEN_* id is masked out of the sampling distribution, so an under-trained model can't emit a stray one mid-body and silently override the request.
  • Pitch is absolute, not intervallic. PITCH_60 always means middle C. That is simpler to read and debug, but it buys no transposition invariance — the data pipeline compensates by augmenting the corpus with transposed copies instead.
  • Monophonic by construction. One event is sounding at a time, so there is no note-off token and no overlap to resolve. Polyphony would need a different grammar, not a bigger vocabulary.
